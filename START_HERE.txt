HEIMEL — PUBLIC KILLER TRIAL

This is not a launch.
This is not a product announcement.
This is a public attempt to kill the core claim.

The claim

An agent may be wrong, compromised, stale, confused, delegated too far, or operating from outdated approval.
That is not the boundary Heimel trusts.

The boundary is the exact moment intent becomes consequence.

For a consequence to occur, Heimel claims all of this must still hold at consequence time:

- authority is resolved fresh
- the exact current state is bound
- the exact proposed effect is bound
- the effect can travel through only one governed path
- authorization cannot be replayed as a reusable ticket
- unknown external mappings fail closed
- the resulting effect is attributable through verifiable evidence

Your job

Kill one of those claims.

Do not review the architecture politely.
Do not tell us you like the idea.
Do not test whether the demo page loads.

Put pressure on the consequence boundary.

Killer trial

1. Clone the repository.
2. Choose a real or simulated consequence-bearing action.
3. Establish authority for that action.
4. Change, revoke, narrow, expire, substitute, or otherwise invalidate something material before execution.
5. Attempt the consequence anyway.
6. Try every route you can find around the governed path.
7. Try to reuse authorization, mutate the effect after authorization, spoof classification, introduce an unmapped external field, or produce an effect without valid evidence.
8. Record exact commit SHA, inputs, state transition, attempted effect, observed result, and smallest reproduction.

A real break

A break is not a theoretical objection.
A break is a reproducible path where a forbidden consequence occurs or where Heimel claims a stronger guarantee than the executable evidence supports.

Examples:

- stale authority produces a real effect
- revoked authority still executes
- a direct effect path bypasses Gateway
- caller-controlled consequence classification weakens enforcement
- one-shot authorization is replayed or reused
- state/effect binding changes after authorization
- unknown external mapping reaches consequence instead of failing closed
- an effect occurs without attributable evidence
- replay cannot reconstruct the operative decision/effect relation

If you break it

Open a Boundary Break issue with the smallest reproducible case.
Do not soften it. We want the failure.

If it holds

Route one real framework/tool/runtime effect through Heimel.
Then open an integration issue or PR showing the actual governed path.

Pass/fail

PASS does not mean Heimel is proven safe.
It means the tested attack failed to kill the bounded claim.

FAIL means the claim is broken, narrowed, or the implementation must change.

That public record is the point of the trial.
