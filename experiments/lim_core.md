# LIM core

Canonical short statement for the Law of Identity Maintenance.

## Research position

LIM does not claim to have discovered a universal law.

LIM does not claim to have solved the problem of identity.

LIM proposes that multiple disciplines may be confronting the same underlying question:

```text
How can stable identity persist through continuous change?
```

The current working hypothesis is:

```text
Stable identity requires invariant-preserving transformation over time.
```

This statement should be understood as a research question as much as a proposition.

If true, it immediately raises further questions:

```text
What is the invariant?
What transformations preserve it?
Which transformations destroy it?
How is preservation detected?
How does the answer differ across domains?
```

The objective of LIM is not to force all domains into a single explanation.

The objective is to investigate whether biology, cognition, language, organizations, societies and artificial systems are confronting different instances of the same underlying problem:

```text
The maintenance of identity through change.
```

The value of LIM may ultimately lie less in providing a final answer and more in providing a common question that can be explored across domains.

## Relation to the core equation

```text
I = Phi(tau)
```

Meaning:

```text
Identity is the function of the Filter over Time.
```

The core statement expands this into operational language:

```text
stable identity = identity that persists without becoming rigid or dissolving
requires = minimum condition for systems that must survive change
invariant-preserving = something essential must be conserved
transformation = the system must still be able to change
over time = identity is maintained recursively, not possessed statically
```

```text
No transformation -> stasis.
No time -> no maintenance.
No filter -> drift.
```
