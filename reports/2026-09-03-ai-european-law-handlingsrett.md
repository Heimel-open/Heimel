# AI in European Law — Handlingsrett / execution-governance signal

Status: external legal evidence / research note  
Date: 2026-09-03  
Owner: VALO Research  
Source: Herwig C. H. Hofmann, Elif Biber, Zahra Yusifli (eds.), *AI in European Law: A Deep View*, Luxembourg Legal Studies Vol. 28, Nomos, 1st ed. 2026.  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.5771/9783748969303

## Why this matters

This volume provides current European legal support for a distinction that is central to Handlingsrett: system-level compliance is not equivalent to action-level authority or accountability.

The strongest overlap is in the book's criticism of the EU AI Act's enforcement architecture. Biber and Hofmann argue that the Act risks conflating conformity with accountability by relying heavily on provider self-assessment and product-safety-style conformity procedures for questions that extend into fundamental rights. Their conclusion is that technical compliance alone cannot make rights operative, contestable, or enforceable.

VALO implication:

> Compliance is a property of a system. Authority is a property of an action in context. Accountability requires evidence of the latter.

A conformity assessment may answer whether a system satisfies specified requirements for deployment. It does not establish that a particular consequential action was authorised for a particular actor, purpose, subject, time, state, and constraint set.

## 1. Conformance is not consequence-time authority

The book's analysis supports a clean separation:

- Conformance: may this system be placed into service or used under a defined regulatory regime?
- Handlingsrett: may this actor or system perform this specific consequential action now, under the authority and constraints that exist at execution time?

This distinction maps directly to the VALO chain:

VAIG -> REHT -> RACS -> Gateway/PEP -> Veritas evidence.

System admissibility can be a prerequisite, but it cannot substitute for fresh action-level authorisation.

## 2. Human oversight is not human control

The final chapter argues that formal human oversight can become a legitimising facade when cognitive, organisational, and structural asymmetries prevent the human from acting as an effective counterweight to the system.

VALO consequence:

Human presence is not sufficient. Human-in-the-Lead requires effective authority to stop, modify, deny, or escalate a consequential action before effect.

Therefore a valid oversight claim should require evidence that the human or authorised control function had:

- visibility of the relevant state,
- competence and mandate,
- time and mechanism to intervene,
- an operative effect-path control,
- evidence that intervention authority was live at consequence time.

## 3. Agreement is not continuing authority to execute

The smart-contract chapter identifies a closely related execution problem. Consent to contractual content does not necessarily amount to informed consent to autonomous execution. Once execution is encoded and automatic, discretion, renegotiation, suspension, and intervention may disappear unless explicitly built into the mechanism.

VALO consequence:

> Agreement to a rule or contract does not create unlimited continuing authority to execute it.

Execution must remain subject to current authority, applicable constraints, revocation, changed state, and legally relevant exceptions. This strengthens the REHT principle that authority is resolved fresh at consequence time rather than inherited indefinitely from an earlier approval.

## 4. Autonomous and networked systems do not erase human accountability

Moraiti's chapter rejects a simple move toward full AI legal personhood, warning that attribution to the AI can become an empty imputation that lets responsible human actors escape scrutiny.

The chapter also distinguishes three forms of machine behaviour:

1. individual autonomous machine behaviour,
2. hybrid human-machine behaviour,
3. collective/networked machine behaviour emerging from interconnected agents.

The third category is especially relevant to VALO's network research: risk and behaviour may arise from the interconnection itself rather than from one isolated model or node.

VALO consequence:

Delegated machine action may carry scoped Handlingsrett without making the machine the ultimate source of authority or accountability. Authority lineage, delegation, constraints, and evidence must remain traceable through networked execution.

## 5. Rights require an operative path to enforcement

Biber and Hofmann's central criticism is that rights language without procedural mechanisms leaves rights insufficiently visible, contestable, and enforceable. They call for explicit legal pathways connecting rights, risks, remedies, independent oversight, and access to justice.

VALO translation:

A right that cannot constrain an execution path is not yet an operative control.

For a governed effect path, rights and legal constraints must be translated into machine-checkable authority and constraint state before consequence. Post-hoc explanation alone is insufficient.

## Architectural adoption

Adopt this source as a central external legal reference for Handlingsrett and execution governance.

Record the following design propositions:

1. CONFORMANCE_NE_AUTHORITY  
   A system-level conformity result must never be treated as sufficient action-level authority.

2. OVERSIGHT_REQUIRES_EFFECTIVE_INTERVENTION  
   Human oversight is valid only where the authorised human/control function can actually interrupt or alter the effect path.

3. PRIOR_CONSENT_NE_CURRENT_EXECUTION_RIGHT  
   Earlier consent, contract, policy approval, or deployment approval does not by itself establish current authority to execute.

4. AUTHORITY_RESOLVED_AT_CONSEQUENCE_TIME  
   Material execution requires fresh evaluation of authority, purpose, constraints, revocation, and current state immediately before effect.

5. NETWORKED_ACTION_REQUIRES_TRACEABLE_AUTHORITY_LINEAGE  
   Multi-agent or collective machine behaviour must not dissolve responsibility. Delegation and authority lineage must remain attributable across the network.

6. RIGHTS_MUST_REACH_THE_EFFECT_PATH  
   Rights and remedies must be capable of constraining, stopping, contesting, or reversing consequential execution where legally possible; they cannot remain descriptive policy language only.
