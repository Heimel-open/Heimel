# AI complementary infrastructure — from wealth to governed action

Status: external evidence / positioning note  
Date: 2026-09-03  
Source: Isabella Loaiza and Roberto Rigobon, *From Wealth to Welfare: The Social and Economic Institutions to Complement AI* / MIT Sloan summary, *Will AI Enhance Welfare for All or Generate Wealth for Just a Few?*

## Source-derived frame

The source treats AI as a general-purpose technology whose benefits depend on complementary system-level infrastructure.

It uses the automobile as an example: mass adoption depended not only on the vehicle itself, but on roads, traffic laws, safety standards, licensure, insurance and related infrastructure.

It contrasts this with the internet, where insufficient investment in governance, public data infrastructure and access policies is presented as one reason benefits were distributed unevenly.

The authors identify five complementary investment areas required to convert innovation-created wealth into welfare:

1. capital;
2. human development;
3. social and economic institutions;
4. metrics;
5. systems thinking.

The source also states that AI should be treated as part of larger social, economic and technical systems rather than as a stand-alone tool.

## VALO interpretation

The source supports the broader complementary-infrastructure thesis, but stops above the execution layer.

For agentic AI, the missing complement is not only policy, regulation or institutional design. Once AI systems can cause real-world effects, a machine-enforceable execution infrastructure is required to determine:

- who may act;
- under which mandate or delegation;
- for which purpose;
- under which constraints;
- whether that authority is still operative at consequence time;
- whether the action may enter the effect path now;
- and how the resulting effect is evidenced.

VALO calls this layer Handlingsrett / execution authority infrastructure.

The analogy can be stated compactly:

- automobile -> vehicle + roads + traffic rules + licensing;
- internet -> network + protocols + identity/access infrastructure;
- agentic AI -> models + compute + connectivity + execution authority infrastructure.

The model is not the system.

A capable agent without a governed effect boundary is analogous to a vehicle without traffic infrastructure: technically operable, but not sufficient for safe, scalable social deployment.

## Architectural implication

This does not require a new VALO architecture.

It strengthens the positioning of the existing chain:

`VAIG -> REHT -> RACS -> governed Gateway/PEP -> Veritas evidence`

as a system-level complement to agentic AI rather than merely an internal governance feature.

The key distinction is:

> Policy can state what should be allowed.  
> Execution authority infrastructure determines whether this concrete action is allowed to take effect now.

That moves the category beyond "AI governance" in the conventional policy/compliance sense.

## Market thesis

If agentic AI becomes a general-purpose execution substrate, execution authority becomes shared infrastructure.

The resulting category is larger than model governance because it applies at the boundary where heterogeneous agents, models, tools, organizations, mandates and real-world effects meet.

The relevant market frame is therefore not only:

`AI governance software`

but:

`authority + admissibility + governed execution + evidence infrastructure for machine action`

## Evidence boundary

The source supports:

- AI as a general-purpose technology;
- the need for complementary infrastructure;
- the importance of governance, institutions, measurement and systems thinking;
- the automobile/internet comparison.

The source does not itself propose commit-time authorization, Handlingsrett, REHT, RACS, governed effect paths or consequence-time authority resolution. Those are VALO interpretations and architecture.
