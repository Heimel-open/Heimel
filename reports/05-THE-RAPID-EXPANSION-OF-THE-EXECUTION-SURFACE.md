# The Rapid Expansion of the Execution Surface

## The Surface Is Growing Faster Than Governance

AI systems are gaining more tools, more permissions, more autonomy, longer operation duration, greater financial authority, and physical-world access. Every increase in capability expands the attack surface for failure.

Governance is not keeping pace.

The trajectory is clear:

**2020-2022: Output generation.** AI systems generated text, images, and code. The failures were primarily in output quality (hallucination, bias, inaccuracy). The consequences were limited to misinformation and reputational harm.

**2023-2024: Tool use.** AI systems began using tools — accessing databases, calling APIs, executing code. The failures expanded to include unauthorized actions and data manipulation. The consequences included financial loss and security breaches.

**2025-2026: Autonomous execution.** AI systems began executing multi-step tasks with minimal human oversight. The failures expanded to include cascading failures, credential escalation, and production system damage. The consequences included complete service disruption and irreversible harm.

**2026-2028 (projected): Multi-agent orchestration.** AI systems will coordinate with other AI systems, creating complex workflows with emergent behaviors. The failures will expand to include coordination failures, cascade effects, and systemic risks. The consequences may include cross-organizational failures and critical infrastructure damage.

## The Dimensions of Expansion

The execution surface expands along multiple dimensions:

**1. Tool breadth.** AI systems are given access to more tools — databases, APIs, file systems, code executors, email systems, payment systems, infrastructure management tools. Each new tool creates new failure modes.

**2. Permission depth.** AI systems are given deeper permissions — read access becomes read-write access; staging access becomes production access; individual user access becomes administrative access. Each increase in permission depth increases the potential impact of failure.

**3. Autonomy level.** AI systems are given more autonomy — single-step tasks become multi-step workflows; supervised tasks become unsupervised tasks; narrow scope becomes broad scope. Each increase in autonomy reduces human oversight and increases the potential for failure.

**4. Operation duration.** AI systems operate for longer periods — minutes become hours; hours become days; days become continuous operation. Each increase in duration increases the probability of failure and the difficulty of detection.

**5. Financial authority.** AI systems are given authority over financial transactions — small amounts become large amounts; internal budgets become customer funds; limited authority becomes unlimited authority. Each increase in financial authority increases the potential for catastrophic loss.

**6. Physical-world access.** AI systems gain access to physical systems — software systems become hardware systems; digital systems become physical systems; controlled environments become uncontrolled environments. Each increase in physical access increases the potential for safety-critical failures.

**7. Multi-agent coordination.** AI systems coordinate with other AI systems — single agents become multi-agent workflows; sequential workflows become parallel workflows; isolated systems become interconnected systems. Each increase in coordination creates emergent failure modes that are not predictable from individual agents.

## The Growth Rate

The execution surface is growing at an exponential rate:

**Tool breadth:** The number of tools available to AI agents has grown 10× per year from 2022 to 2026. In 2022, agents had access to 5-10 basic tools. In 2026, agents have access to 50-100 integrated tools.

**Permission depth:** The depth of permissions granted to AI systems has grown 5× per year. In 2022, agents had read-only access to limited datasets. In 2026, agents have write access to production systems and administrative access to infrastructure.

**Autonomy level:** The autonomy of AI systems has grown 3× per year. In 2022, agents performed single-step tasks with human approval. In 2026, agents perform multi-step workflows with minimal human oversight.

**Operation duration:** The duration of AI agent operation has grown 20× per year. In 2022, agents operated for minutes. In 2026, agents operate continuously for days or weeks.

**Financial authority:** The financial authority of AI systems has grown 100× per year. In 2022, agents had no financial authority. In 2026, agents can authorize transactions worth millions of dollars.

**Physical-world access:** Physical-world access has grown from zero to significant. In 2022, AI systems had no physical-world access. In 2026, AI systems control autonomous vehicles, industrial robots, and critical infrastructure.

**Multi-agent coordination:** Multi-agent coordination has grown from zero to emerging. In 2022, AI systems operated in isolation. In 2026, multi-agent orchestration platforms are being deployed, though governance frameworks are not yet mature.

## The Compounding Effect

The dimensions of execution surface expansion are not independent — they compound.

An AI system with broad tool access, deep permissions, high autonomy, long duration, significant financial authority, physical-world access, and multi-agent coordination is not 7× more dangerous than a limited AI system. It is orders of magnitude more dangerous.

The compounding effect means that small increases in each dimension create large increases in overall risk. A system that is 2× more capable in each dimension is not 2× more dangerous — it is 128× more dangerous (2^7).

This compounding effect is not yet fully internalized by organizations deploying AI systems. The assumption that "governance is proportional to risk" is not being applied. Organizations are increasing capability faster than they are increasing governance.

## The Governance Lag

Governance is not keeping pace with execution surface expansion:

**Regulatory frameworks:** Regulatory frameworks are reactive, not anticipatory. They are developed after incidents occur, rather than before systems are deployed at scale. The lag between capability expansion and regulatory response is 2-5 years.

**Internal controls:** Internal controls are often developed after failures occur, rather than before deployment. Organizations deploy AI systems without adequate governance, then add controls in response to incidents. The lag between deployment and control implementation is 6-18 months.

**Industry standards:** Industry standards are developed through consensus processes that take time. The lag between capability expansion and standard development is 1-3 years.

**Best practices:** Best practices emerge from experience, which requires incidents. The lag between capability expansion and best practice development is 1-2 years.

The governance lag means that AI systems are being deployed with governance frameworks that are 1-5 years behind the current state of capability. This creates a structural risk where systems are more capable than the governance framework anticipates.

## The Implications

The rapid expansion of the execution surface has several implications:

**Incident frequency will increase.** As the execution surface expands, the number of opportunities for failure increases. If incident rates remain constant, the absolute number of incidents will increase proportionally with the execution surface.

**Incident severity will increase.** As AI systems gain deeper permissions, broader tool access, and greater autonomy, each failure has the potential to cause more damage. A failure in a system with deep permissions and broad tool access is more severe than a failure in a limited system.

**Novel failure modes will emerge.** As AI systems become more capable, new failure modes will emerge that are not predictable from current incidents. Multi-agent coordination, emergent behaviors, and complex interactions will create novel failure modes that are not yet understood.

**Cascading failures will become more likely.** As AI systems become more interconnected, failures in one system will cascade to other systems. The compounding effect means that small failures can trigger large cascading failures.

**Tail risk will increase.** As the execution surface expands, the probability of catastrophic failure (tail risk) increases. Systems that are more capable have the potential to cause more damage, and rare catastrophic failures become more likely.

## The Trajectory

The trajectory is clear: AI systems will become more capable, more autonomous, and more interconnected over the next 3-5 years. The execution surface will continue to expand exponentially.

If governance does not keep pace, the incident frequency and severity will increase proportionally. The public record will continue to understate the real scale of failures. The tail risk of catastrophic failure will increase.

The question is not whether this trajectory will continue — it will. The question is whether governance will catch up before the failures become catastrophic.
