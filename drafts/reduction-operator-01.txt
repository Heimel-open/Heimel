REDUCTION OPERATOR — RESEARCH BRANCH 01
Date: 2026-09-11
Status: OPEN / FALSIFIABLE
Owner: Njål

PRIMARY QUESTION
Is there a domain-general problem-solving operator in which performance improves by progressively removing solved, irrelevant, redundant or non-material structure until the residual mechanism becomes observable?

WORKING OPERATOR
DECOMPOSE → SORT → REMOVE → ISOLATE RESIDUAL → IDENTIFY MECHANISM → RECONSTRUCT MINIMUM SUFFICIENT SOLUTION

The claim is not that simplification is always good. The claim under test is narrower: when decomposition preserves material relations, removing what is already resolved or non-causal may expose a smaller residual in which the governing mechanism is easier to identify.

KNOWN INSTANCES / MOTIVATING OBSERVATIONS
1. Singapore Math / number bonds: decompose into easier chunks, resolve them, recombine.
2. Saint-Exupéry / design: completion by removal rather than accumulation.
3. Njål-method: remove argument pressure and excess explanation; expose mechanism and reality contact; let the receiver reconstruct the conclusion.
4. Cross-domain 'bloodhound' observations: finance, AI/model harnesses, money/economics, identity, engineering, GCU, need→outcome, Suno→TV. Recurrent move: remove domain surface detail, identify available components and missing relation, expose smallest operative product/mechanism.

PROPOSITION ROP-P01
For a non-trivial problem P with separable or conditionally separable structure, iterative removal of components classified as RESOLVED, IRRELEVANT or REDUNDANT will reduce search complexity without materially reducing solution quality, provided causal/material relations crossing the removed boundary are preserved.

PROPOSITION ROP-P02
The residual after valid reduction contains a higher concentration of decision-relevant structure than the unreduced representation.

PROPOSITION ROP-P03
If the operator is domain-general, trained use should transfer across domains better than domain-specific heuristics alone on structurally novel tasks.

NON-CLAIMS
- Reduction is not compression at any cost.
- Relationships may not be discarded merely because nodes/components look individually redundant.
- The shortest representation is not necessarily the best representation.
- A locally simple answer that destroys the global objective is a failure.

FAILURE / FALSIFICATION CONDITIONS
P01 narrows or fails if reduction reliably removes material dependencies, increases error, or does not reduce effective search cost relative to controls.
P02 fails if residuals do not show greater decision-relevant density under blinded evaluation.
P03 fails if performance gains remain confined to trained domains or familiar task forms.
The general claim must be narrowed if success depends mainly on highly decomposable arithmetic/design tasks.

METHOD — REDUCTION OPERATOR PROTOCOL v0.1

For each task freeze before execution:
A. Primary objective and success criterion.
B. Full initial problem representation.
C. Material-dependency map: relations that must survive reduction.
D. Prospective falsification criterion.
E. Cost measures: time, steps/actions, tokens where applicable, errors, reversals.
F. Outcome-quality measure independent of elegance/shortness.

Reduction pass:
1. DECOMPOSE: identify candidate components without solving the whole task.
2. SORT each component: UNRESOLVED / RESOLVED / IRRELEVANT / REDUNDANT / DEPENDENCY-BEARING / UNKNOWN.
3. REMOVE only RESOLVED, IRRELEVANT or REDUNDANT components whose removal does not sever a material dependency. UNKNOWN stays.
4. ISOLATE RESIDUAL: freeze what remains. No new assumptions may be silently introduced.
5. IDENTIFY MECHANISM: state the smallest causal/operational account sufficient to explain or solve the residual.
6. RECONSTRUCT: add back only information required for a correct executable/communicable solution.
7. VERIFY against the original objective and dependency map.

CONTROL CONDITIONS
C0: normal unconstrained problem solving.
C1: additive analysis — gather/expand information before solving.
C2: decomposition only — break into parts but do not explicitly remove resolved/non-material structure.
C3: reduction operator.

BLINDING
Where feasible, one agent/person executes and another scores without knowing condition. Evaluation uses frozen success criteria, not persuasiveness or perceived simplicity.

RUN SERIES
ROP-R01 — Arithmetic / structured reasoning
Purpose: establish that the protocol reproduces the obvious Singapore-Math-style advantage where expected, and validate instrumentation. Use matched multi-step arithmetic/algebra tasks. This is calibration, not evidence for domain-generality.

ROP-R02 — Logic / constraint problem
Use unfamiliar constraint-satisfaction tasks with decoy facts and cross-dependencies. Test whether reduction removes decoys without deleting necessary relations.

ROP-R03 — Engineering diagnosis
Provide system incidents with mixed causal and non-causal telemetry. Measure diagnosis accuracy, time/steps and whether dependency-bearing evidence is preserved.

ROP-R04 — Product discovery
Give component/ecosystem descriptions without proposed product. Example class: capability A + surface B + control C with a missing connective layer. Compare novelty, feasibility and minimum sufficient product definition under blinded scoring.

ROP-R05 — Communication / sales
Use cases where sender and receiver models differ. Compare persuasive/additive communication with mechanism/reality-contact reduction. Primary outcome is receiver's independent reconstruction and decision quality, not agreement.

ROP-R06 — Research hypothesis
Give noisy evidence bundles. Test whether the operator improves identification of the smallest surviving proposition and explicit UNKNOWNs without overstating evidence.

ROP-R07 — Adversarial relational task
Construct tasks where individually removable-looking elements encode critical relationships. This run is designed to kill naive reduction. Success requires retaining relation-bearing structure even when surface components appear redundant.

ROP-R08 — Cross-domain transfer
Train/familiarize participants only on one domain, then test on unseen domains. This is the main test of P03.

ROP-R09 — Complexity threshold
Vary task size and coupling. Identify where reduction helps, becomes neutral, or fails. Estimate boundary conditions rather than forcing universality.

ROP-R10 — Reconstruction ablation
Compare residual-only solution with residual + minimum reconstruction and full original representation. Tests whether reconstruction is a necessary stage rather than rhetorical cleanup.

PRIMARY METRICS
Outcome correctness/quality.
Material dependency preservation.
Effective search cost: time + actions/steps + reversals; tokens as secondary machine measure.
Residual decision-relevant density.
Calibration: confidence versus correctness.
Transfer performance on unseen domains.

STOP RULES
Stop and record FAILURE when a run's prospective kill criterion is met. Do not repair the proposition after seeing the result inside the same run.
Stop reduction immediately when the next removal has UNKNOWN materiality.
Do not promote an attractive anecdote to evidence of domain-generality.
Three positive domains do not establish universality; adversarial and transfer runs are mandatory before strengthening the claim.

FIRST ACTIVE RUN — ROP-R01
Goal: validate protocol mechanics, classifications and scoring on a domain where decomposition is already known to work.
Expected result: C3 should reduce effective search cost without reducing correctness relative to C0/C2.
Kill criterion for protocol implementation: if independent scorers cannot consistently distinguish the component classes or reproduce the dependency-preservation decision, protocol v0.1 is not operational enough to proceed to cross-domain claims.
Evidence to preserve: frozen task set, condition assignment, initial representations, each removal decision, residual snapshots, final answers, metrics, scorer outputs.

NEXT GATE
Execute ROP-R01. If protocol instrumentation is usable, proceed directly to ROP-R02 and ROP-R07 in parallel: one normal generalization test and one adversarial kill test. Do not wait for a narrative synthesis.
