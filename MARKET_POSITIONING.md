## Competitive Positioning

### vs AEGIS (Pre-execution firewall)
- **AEGIS:** "Is this tool call dangerous?"
- **VAIG:** "Is this decision epistemically legitimate?"
- **Relationship:** Complementary. Layer 3 (tool safety) vs Layer 5 (decision legitimacy).
- **Strategy:** Position as "AEGIS prevents bad actions. VAIG prevents bad decisions."

### vs TrigGuard (Execution authorization)
- **TrigGuard:** "Does policy permit this action?"
- **VAIG:** "Should the agent have decided this, given evidence + authority?"
- **Relationship:** Adjacent. Authorization (policy) vs Admissibility (evidence-based).
- **Strategy:** "TrigGuard enforces rules. VAIG enforces reasoning."

### vs Right-to-Act (Formal admissibility)
- **Right-to-Act:** "Formal framework for admissible decisions"
- **VAIG:** "Operational, runtime-enforced admissibility"
- **Relationship:** Same problem space. Different maturity.
- **Strategy:** "Right-to-Act owns the theory. VAIG owns the practice."

### vs Compliance Platforms (Credo, OneTrust)
- **Credo/OneTrust:** "Are your policies compliant?"
- **VAIG:** "Are your decisions admissible?"
- **Relationship:** Orthogonal layers. Together = verifiable governance.
- **Strategy:** "They ensure governance exists. VAIG ensures governance executes."
