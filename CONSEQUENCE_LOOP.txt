HEIMEL CONSEQUENCE LOOP

Enterprise control over the learning loop is not enough.
You also need control over the consequence loop.

Human control cannot mean a human approves every action.
It has to mean human authority remains operative when the action becomes consequential.

Canonical path:
model/agent -> GCU/work contract -> Heimel consequence-time authority -> governed effect -> evidence

Heimel distinction:
Model governance, learning-loop ownership, embedded evaluation, and policy controls are necessary but insufficient. The decisive control point is consequence time: who actually has authority when an action becomes capable of producing an external effect.

Operational rule:
Authority must be resolved fresh at consequence time. No model, agent, tool, workflow, or external system receives a direct effect path around that check.

Core formulation:
When a human can no longer keep a finger on every button, authority, boundaries, and responsibility must follow the button itself.

External convergence:
Recent enterprise AI-control arguments increasingly emphasize retained human control, ownership of the learning loop, and stronger control mechanisms around models, knowledge, and evaluation. Heimel extends this to the consequence boundary: control must remain operative at the moment an action becomes consequential.
