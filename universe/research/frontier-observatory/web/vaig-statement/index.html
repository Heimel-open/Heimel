VΛLΦ Research Group — Position Statement — 2026

VAIG is not a guardrail.

VAIG is an admissibility layer.

Guardrails react to bad output. They let the system act, observe what happened, and then block or warn after the fact. The damage window is already open.

Admissibility decides whether action is allowed before damage occurs. It is a gate on actions, not a filter on outputs. The decision happens at the boundary, not after crossing it.

Guardrail
Action → Output → Check → Block or warn
Admissibility
Action proposed → Evaluate → Allow or deny
When
Guardrail: post-hoc. Admissibility: pre-emptive.
What
Guardrail: filter on outputs. Admissibility: gate on actions.
VAIG
Admissibility layer. Not a guardrail.

The distinction is architectural, not cosmetic. A system built around admissibility is structurally different from one built around guardrails — it does not require observation of failure in order to prevent it.

VAIG (Valo AI Governance) is an 8-instrument ensemble with a unified distrust engine, a WORM audit log, and pre-execution decision gates grounded in the Φ-Law: a universal stability threshold derived from number theory and chaos theory.
