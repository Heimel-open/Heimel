# LIM consilience assessment — informal internal note

Date: 2026-07-01
Status: internal / private working note

## Context

This note evaluates a set of external conceptual references against LIM / Φ-loven:

- hierarchy of physical theories
- Einsteinian geometry to symmetry dynamics
- Lorenz / chaos theory
- coherence as a wave function
- materials science equation hierarchy

These are not treated as evidence for LIM. They are treated as analogical or structural reference points.

## Assessment

### 1. Hierarchy of physical theories

Strength against LIM: high as analogy.

The useful principle is that higher-level theories do not necessarily reject lower-level theories. They reinterpret them as special cases under narrower conditions. This maps cleanly to LIM if LIM is framed as a meta-principle of identity maintenance and stability, not as a replacement for physics.

Use: conceptual framing.
Do not use: empirical support.

### 2. Einsteinian geometry to symmetry dynamics

Strength against LIM: moderate.

The relevant parallel is the shift from static geometry to more fundamental process or symmetry structure. This resembles the LIM move from static identity to maintained identity through filtering over time.

However, this is speculative as physics. It should not be used as support for LIM unless the underlying physical model itself is independently established.

Use: philosophical analogy.
Do not use: scientific evidence.

### 3. Chaos theory / Lorenz

Strength against LIM: very high.

This is the strongest scientific analogue. It gives established language for:

- dynamic systems
- attractors
- sensitivity to initial conditions
- nonlinear evolution
- stability regions
- state-space behaviour

Lorenz describes the dynamics. LIM asks what kind of filtering or invariant-preserving transformation is required for identity to survive through those dynamics.

Use: core adjacent literature.
Do not overclaim: Lorenz does not prove LIM.

### 4. Coherence as a wave function

Strength against LIM: moderate.

The conceptual overlap is clear: coherence, semantic fields, interference, evolution and governance. But the material appears closer to a speculative or conceptual model than a validated mathematical framework.

Use: inspiration / parallel vocabulary.
Do not use: evidence.

### 5. Materials science equations

Strength against LIM: high as structural lesson.

The useful point is not that materials science proves LIM. It is that mature scientific fields often stabilize around a small number of precise equations that explain, predict and constrain system behaviour.

For LIM, this implies a discipline requirement:

- one core equation
- explicit axioms
- derived consequences
- measurable variables
- falsification conditions

Use: methodological model.
Do not use: domain support.

## Short synthesis

The shared pattern across these references is not physics itself. The shared pattern is dynamic stability.

Across chaos theory, physical theory hierarchies, coherence models and engineering disciplines, stable systems are not defined only by state. They are defined by how state is preserved, transformed or constrained through time.

This aligns with the LIM core:

> Stable identity requires invariant-preserving transformation over time.

## Boundary condition

These references can support the language and framing of LIM, but they do not validate LIM.

For LIM to stand as a theory rather than a synthesis, it must produce at least one of the following:

1. a mathematical derivation that is not merely a restatement of known fixed-point or control theory,
2. a new measurable quantity with reproducible predictive power,
3. a falsifiable prediction that existing frameworks would not naturally produce,
4. an operational architecture that demonstrably improves governance, stability or auditability in real AI systems.

## Current conclusion

LIM is strongest when framed as a synthesis of dynamic systems, control, filtering, spectral coherence and governance.

It becomes a stronger independent theory only if it predicts new observable phenomena that are later confirmed under controlled conditions.
