# Müller: autonomy, opacity, resistance and AI risk

Status: M1 conceptual / external research grounding
Date: 2026-08-14

Purpose: record external philosophical support relevant to the AI Collaboration Boundary Manifesto without promoting the manifesto above M1 or treating conceptual alignment as empirical validation.

## Relevant primary-source positions

### Deep opacity

Vincent C. Müller (2025), "Deep Opacity in AI: A Threat to XAI and Standard Privacy Protection Mechanisms", distinguishes shallow opacity, standard black-box opacity and "deep opacity": cases where analysts cannot possibly know what a system might do.

Relevance to the manifesto: if internal behavior can become deeply opaque, safety cannot depend only on complete prediction or explanation of the intelligent system. The manifesto therefore places a separate, explicit boundary between intelligent proposal and external consequence.

This architectural inference is ours; Müller does not claim or validate the VALO/REHT design.

### Autonomy and control

Müller (2012), "Autonomous cognitive systems in real-world environments: Less control, more flexibility and better interaction", argues that autonomy is relative and graded; increasing autonomy from makers and users is a necessary feature of increasingly intelligent systems operating in the real world; and greater autonomy means less control while enabling improved interaction.

Relevance to the manifesto: increasing cognitive autonomy does not have to imply ungoverned external consequence. The design thesis separates freedom to reason/propose from legitimate authority to act.

### Interaction and resistance

Müller (2011), "Interaction and resistance: The recognition of intentions in new human-computer interaction", argues that interaction between intelligent agents involves recognizing intentions partly through resistance to carrying out another agent's intentions.

Relevance to the manifesto: refusal, defer, dissent, step-up and other forms of bounded resistance can be legitimate interaction outcomes rather than system failures. Structured collaboration need not mean unconditional obedience.

### Existential risk and badly designed instrumental AI

Vincent C. Müller and Michael Cannon (2022), "Existential risk from AI and orthogonality: Can we have it both ways?", argue that the standard existential-risk argument combining a singularity claim with the orthogonality thesis is not sound under their analysis. They explicitly retain the possibility of very significant harm from instrumental AI that is badly designed or badly used.

Relevance to the manifesto: this is consistent with focusing governance on concrete consequence, authority and use rather than treating intelligence itself as the hazard.

## Synthesis

Taken together, these works provide independent conceptual support for four parts of the collaboration-boundary thesis:

1. intelligence may become difficult or impossible to fully predict internally;
2. greater real-world intelligence is associated with greater autonomy and therefore reduced direct control;
3. legitimate interaction between agents can include resistance rather than obedience;
4. serious AI harm can arise from design and use without requiring a claim that intelligence itself is inherently dangerous.

The resulting VALO/Tofoo inference is:

Free the intelligence. Govern the consequence.

This is an architectural and normative inference, not a claim that Müller endorses VALO, REHT or the manifesto.

## Limits

Epistemic status remains M1 conceptual support.

These sources do not empirically validate VALO or REHT, do not establish that current AI is conscious or morally considerable, do not establish that ASI exists or is safe, and do not establish any Fermi-related hypothesis. They support the problem framing and the separation between intelligent autonomy and governed consequence.
