# Cognitive seeds

Cognitive seeds are small, versioned ideas that other people and projects can
adopt, test, extend or reject.

They are deliberately lighter than standards and more disciplined than loose
ideas. A seed should state:

- the proposition or vocabulary being offered;
- what problem it helps explain or measure;
- the minimum structure needed to try it;
- observable adoption tests and falsifiers;
- what it must not be used to claim; and
- provenance, version and attribution.

## Boundary

A seed does not become a protocol, runtime, product, authorization mechanism or
source of truth merely because it is published or adopted. Adoption is local,
explicit and reversible. Independent implementations should preserve the seed
version and record where they diverge.

## Orientation

The [orientation guide](ORIENTATION.md) and [machine-readable catalogue](catalog.yaml)
define the stable vocabulary, status labels and relationship rules an adopting
system can use when representing these seeds. They are an adoption aid, not a
remote instruction channel or authority claim.

The following remain reports or candidate seeds rather than promoted seeds:

- finance-operation simulations and exception cases — domain evidence;
- the Governing Core — architecture/report surface;
- the Solland Paradox and infrastructure debt mechanism — named research
  hypotheses requiring broader independent validation.

The linked reports contain the broader research and evidence surface. These seed
cards are intentionally concise and may be falsified or narrowed as evidence
improves.
