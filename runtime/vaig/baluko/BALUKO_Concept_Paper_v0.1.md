# BALUKO Concept Paper v0.1

Date: 2026-06-17

Status: Working concept

Author: Njål Gaute Solland / VALO Research Group

## Short definition

BALUKO is a framework for monitoring latent system risk.

It is not a governance gate.

It is not a decision engine.

It is not an intelligence system.

It is closer to meteorology for complex systems.

BALUKO observes measurable conditions that historically increase the probability of future systemic failure.

It does not say: failure will happen.

It says: the system is beginning to resemble conditions under which failure has often occurred before.

## Core idea

Most systems detect failure too late.

Organizations measure incidents.

Markets measure prices.

Security teams measure breaches.

Healthcare systems measure outcomes.

Societies measure crises after they become visible.

BALUKO asks a different question:

What conditions are drying out before the fire starts?

The forest fire analogy is central.

A fire index does not detect fire.

It detects dryness, heat, wind, vegetation and other conditions that make fire more likely.

BALUKO applies the same logic to complex systems.

## Name and status

BALUKO is a new working term.

It does not appear to be an established academic concept.

The components exist in different fields, but the proposed combination appears distinct.

The closest related areas are:

Early warning signals

Systemic risk

Resilience engineering

Key risk indicators

OSINT risk monitoring

Complex systems theory

Financial contagion analysis

Supply-chain risk monitoring

Cyber threat exposure monitoring

But BALUKO attempts to generalize the pattern across domains.

## Boundary to VAIG and MECHA

VAIG asks:

May this action occur?

MECHA asks:

Who has standing to approve, stop, re-scope or override?

BALUKO asks:

What latent conditions are building up that may make future failure more likely?

VAIG is local.

MECHA is local.

BALUKO is environmental.

VAIG is the execution boundary.

MECHA is the human authority layer.

BALUKO is the weather report.

It does not decide.

It warns.

## What BALUKO measures

BALUKO should not measure abstract concepts directly.

It should not measure truth, identity, wisdom, legitimacy, culture or meaning directly.

Those are too broad and too easy to politicize.

BALUKO should measure observable proxies.

Candidate indicators:

Source diversity

Representation depth

Feedback latency

Dependency concentration

Centralization

Redundancy

Challenge rate

Reality contact

Capital migration

Infrastructure migration

Coordination density

Actor convergence

Observability

Recovery capacity

These are measurable enough to build a model.

## Latent system drought

The core metaphor is drought.

Drought is not the fire.

Drought is the condition that makes fire more likely.

In complex systems, drought can appear as:

Low diversity

High centralization

Weak feedback

Low redundancy

High dependency

Poor observability

Delayed correction

Loss of direct reality contact

Reduced local knowledge

Over-reliance on a single model, supplier, platform, language or market

BALUKO detects this drying process.

## Internal layer

The internal layer measures the condition of the system itself.

Examples:

How many independent sources does the system use?

How many layers exist between observation and decision?

How quickly does feedback arrive?

How much redundancy remains?

How concentrated are key dependencies?

How often are assumptions challenged?

How much of the system still has direct contact with reality?

This layer is domain-specific but structurally comparable across domains.

## External layer

The external layer searches for patterns in adjacent domains and historical analogues.

It does not claim that two systems are the same.

It searches for structural similarity.

Examples of external comparison fields:

Forest fire risk

Epidemiology

Financial contagion

Supply-chain collapse

Cybersecurity exposure

Aviation safety

Healthcare overload

Power-grid instability

Ecological collapse

Institutional failure

Language and culture loss

The external layer acts as a hypothesis generator.

It should not determine action.

It should say:

This pattern resembles a known fragility pattern from another field.

Investigate further.

## Flow layer

BALUKO should not only measure static conditions.

It should measure movement.

Many systems reveal future stress through migration before formal reports appear.

Capital moves before strategy is published.

People move before organizations explain why.

Infrastructure is built before narratives catch up.

Large actors often reveal more through allocation than through language.

Signals:

Where is capital moving?

Which sectors are being funded?

Which sectors are being abandoned?

Where are data centers being built?

Where is grid infrastructure expanding?

Where are energy contracts changing?

Where are supply chains relocating?

Where are experts being hired?

Where are firms acquiring capacity?

Where are states stockpiling?

The point is not to infer secret intent.

The point is to measure observable movement.

## Coordination layer

A further layer can measure coordination.

This is not espionage.

It can rely on public information.

Examples:

Public conference attendance

Published speaker lists

Corporate event participation

Board memberships

M&A activity

Joint ventures

Research collaborations

Public procurement

Capital allocation

Patent activity

Infrastructure permits

Shipping and logistics data

Public aviation and maritime movement where legally available

The scientific rule:

Measure coordination.

Do not invent motive.

Measure flow.

Do not tell stories.

Measure pattern.

Do not claim conspiracy.

BALUKO should remain a measurement system, not a narrative machine.

## Public information principle

BALUKO does not require secret information.

Its value is based on public signals that are often ignored because they are scattered across domains.

Open signals may include:

Financial flows

Public filings

Procurement records

Conference schedules

Job postings

Infrastructure permits

Shipping data

Energy market data

Patent filings

Academic publications

Press releases

Satellite-observable infrastructure where legally accessible

Public regulatory filings

The point is aggregation, not secrecy.

BALUKO is not intelligence collection.

It is structured public risk meteorology.

## Meteorology analogy

Meteorology does not know the future.

It observes current conditions and compares them with historical patterns.

Temperature

Pressure

Wind

Humidity

Cloud formation

Historical weather systems

BALUKO does the same for complex systems.

Examples:

Centralization

Diversity

Feedback latency

Dependency concentration

Capital movement

Infrastructure movement

Coordination density

Historical failure patterns

A BALUKO report should not say:

This will happen.

It should say:

The observed conditions increasingly resemble prior conditions associated with this class of failure.

## Identity drought

Identity drought is a possible BALUKO sub-index for societies.

It should not ask:

Does the nation have identity?

That is not measurable in a clean way.

It should ask:

Are the mechanisms that transmit identity weakening?

Possible indicators:

Language continuity

Local media density

Local cultural production

Historical knowledge

Institutional trust

Civic participation

Intergenerational transmission

Regional variation

Local traditions

Cultural redundancy

BALUKO should not become ideological.

It should not define what identity should be.

It should measure whether identity-bearing mechanisms are drying out.

## Language and representation loss

A major conversation thread was language.

The core observation:

Language is not representation-neutral.

A model trained primarily on English may perform well in Norwegian, German, Japanese, Arabic or Chinese without fully preserving the semantic, cultural, historical, legal or institutional context carried by those languages.

The issue is not only translation.

The issue is meaning preservation.

A governance chain may work correctly while the original meaning was already degraded before evidence was evaluated.

Possible chain:

Reality -> Representation -> Evidence -> Intent -> Authorization -> Execution

Most governance systems begin at evidence.

BALUKO asks whether the representation step is already drying out.

This applies to all languages.

Not only Norwegian.

Examples:

Nynorsk and regional terms

Sami languages

Japanese politeness and hierarchy

Arabic honor and social terms

Common law legal concepts

Norwegian administrative language

Institutional phrases with historical context

A term such as “framleis” is not merely “still”.

It carries register, region, cultural placement and linguistic identity.

BALUKO can treat representation loss as latent risk.

## Research question

The central research question:

Can latent system conditions be measured in a domain-independent way that provides actionable warning before observable failure emerges?

More specific:

Do similar drought indicators appear before failure across different systems?

Examples:

Finance

Cybersecurity

Healthcare

Energy

Organizations

AI systems

Societies

If yes, BALUKO may become a general-purpose framework for monitoring systemic fragility.

If no, BALUKO should become a family of domain-specific indices.

## Falsifiable core

BALUKO must remain falsifiable.

A BALUKO model should make claims such as:

Systems with rising centralization, falling diversity, increasing feedback latency and declining redundancy show higher probability of later observable failure than comparable systems without those changes.

This can be tested.

The model can fail.

It should not claim universal coherence.

It should not explain everything.

It should identify measurable preconditions that can be compared historically.

## Possible BALUKO score

A simple early model:

BALUKO Risk = f(
  source_diversity,
  representation_depth,
  feedback_latency,
  dependency_concentration,
  centralization,
  redundancy,
  challenge_rate,
  reality_contact,
  capital_migration,
  coordination_density
)

Each domain would define its own measurable proxies.

The score should represent systemic drying, not imminent collapse.

## Global BALUKO Energy Report

Energy was used as a test case.

Core finding:

The world does not primarily have an energy production problem.

The world has an energy flow robustness problem.

Global energy risk appears as:

Chokepoint concentration

Low redundancy

Geopolitical correlation

Grid bottlenecks

AI-driven electricity demand

LNG dependency

Critical component concentration

### Europe

Pattern:

Reduced dependence on Russian energy, but increased exposure to LNG markets, grid constraints, imported components and AI electricity demand.

BALUKO interpretation:

Robust but externally exposed.

Primary drought indicators:

LNG dependency

Grid bottlenecks

Chinese inverter and component exposure

AI data-center electricity growth

Energy sovereignty gap

### United States

Pattern:

Higher energy sovereignty, but rising grid and data-center pressure.

BALUKO interpretation:

Lower import risk than Europe or Asia, but rising domestic capacity and infrastructure stress.

Primary drought indicators:

Regional grid congestion

Data-center demand

AI electricity growth

Aging infrastructure

Model concentration in financial and digital infrastructure

### Asia

Pattern:

High energy import dependence and high exposure to maritime chokepoints.

China adds property debt, local-government debt and industrial energy demand.

Japan adds interest-rate normalization after decades of low rates.

BALUKO interpretation:

High latent risk.

Primary drought indicators:

Hormuz dependence

Energy import dependence

China debt linkage

Japan rate transition

Industrial energy intensity

### Africa

Pattern:

Resource potential but low energy infrastructure resilience.

BALUKO interpretation:

High structural vulnerability.

Primary drought indicators:

Low grid coverage

Weak capital access

High energy poverty

Import dependency in some countries

Low buffer capacity

Data and representation gaps

### Middle East

Pattern:

The world’s largest energy source region is also one of the world’s most important energy chokepoint regions.

BALUKO interpretation:

Very high latent system risk.

Primary drought indicators:

Strait of Hormuz concentration

Oil and gas export concentration

Water-energy dependency

Food import dependency

Geopolitical escalation risk

Low physical redundancy

### Russia

Pattern:

Energy abundance combined with sanctions, market access limits, technology restrictions and reduced observability.

BALUKO interpretation:

High risk with low visibility.

Primary drought indicators:

Buyer concentration

Sanctions exposure

Technology dependency

Payment and clearing constraints

Low transparency

War-economy distortion

## Global energy conclusion

The global BALUKO energy signal is not shortage.

It is concentration.

Too many energy flows, economies and digital systems depend on too few corridors, suppliers, infrastructures and assumptions.

Energy drought means declining robustness in energy flow.

## What appears new

The individual ingredients are not new.

Early warning systems exist.

Systemic risk exists.

Resilience engineering exists.

OSINT risk monitoring exists.

Financial contagion models exist.

Cyber exposure tools exist.

What appears less common is the combination:

A general meteorological framework for complex systems that tracks latent drought conditions across domains using public signals, flow patterns, historical analogues and domain-specific proxies.

This is the potential BALUKO contribution.

## Practical MVP

A first BALUKO MVP should be narrow.

Recommended first domain:

Energy risk in Europe.

Why:

Data exists.

Energy is measurable.

Chokepoints are real.

Capital flow is observable.

Grid constraints are measurable.

AI electricity demand is rising.

A simple MVP could track:

LNG dependency

Grid investment

Transformer backlog

Data-center energy demand

Energy import concentration

Critical component dependency

Electricity price volatility

Conference and policy attention

Capital migration into energy infrastructure

Output:

Monthly BALUKO Energy Drought Report.

Not prediction.

Risk weather.

## Final formulation

BALUKO is a meteorology of complex systems.

It measures drying conditions before visible failure.

It uses open signals, historical analogues and measurable proxies.

It does not decide.

It does not claim hidden knowledge.

It does not infer motive.

It tells decision-makers when the system is becoming dry enough that sparks matter more.
