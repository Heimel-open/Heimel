## 1. Working title

From Refusal to Governable State: Execution-Boundary Governance and Legitimacy Preservation Across Human-AI Transitions

## 2. Submission thesis

Classification should follow the execution path, not only the product label.

The key risk is not only that an AI system gives an answer. The key risk is that an AI system changes what can happen next inside a product, enterprise platform, closed ecosystem or high-consequence workflow.

## 3. Core contribution

1. Separate decision legitimacy, execution legitimacy and transition legitimacy.
2. Treat the Refusal Object as the observable artifact of blocked execution.
3. Use Authority Visibility to separate real operator standing from passive presence in the loop.
4. Use a receiving-side coherence test to check whether refusal returns a governable state.
5. Keep Governability Architecture bounded: it monitors transitions without becoming a new decision mechanism.

## 5. Refusal Object

A refusal is not a terminal denial message.

A refusal is a transition artifact.

At concept level, a Refusal Object should make visible:

- what was blocked
- why it was blocked
- what boundary condition mattered
- what remains uncertain
- who appears to have or lack authority
- what next actions are legitimate
- whether escalation, recovery or contestability remains available
- what receipt preserves auditability and accountability

## 6. Authority Visibility

Human oversight is not satisfied by human presence alone.

Authority Visibility asks whether the receiving human has standing to authorize, challenge, stop, re-scope, escalate or review.

## 8. Article 6 framing

For systems embedded in products, enterprise platforms or closed ecosystems, assessment should consider runtime role in the execution path, not only product label or user-facing description.

Where an AI system blocks, defers, escalates, re-scopes or otherwise mediates an action in a regulated or high-consequence workflow, assessment should consider whether the system preserves human authority, auditability, accountability and contestability across the transition.
