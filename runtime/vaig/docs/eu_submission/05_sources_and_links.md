## Purpose

This file keeps the EU submission package anchored to primary sources.

Use official EU sources where available. Do not rely on secondary media for deadline, legal basis, submission requirements or the scope of the consultation.

### 2. Draft Commission guidelines page

European Commission, Shaping Europe’s digital future.

Title: Draft Commission guidelines on the classification of high-risk AI systems

URL: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/draft-commission-guidelines-classification-high-risk-ai-systems

- official library page for the draft guidelines
- states that the guidelines support providers, deployers and competent market surveillance authorities in assessing whether an AI system should be classified as high-risk
- states that the guidelines facilitate uniform application and effective enforcement of Article 6 AI Act

- provides separate downloads for general principles, Annex I and Annex III material

- Article 6 framing
- high-risk classification structure
- guideline purpose
- Annex I / Annex III pathway references

### 3. EU AI Act legal text

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.

Title: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 of the European Parliament and of the Council laying down harmonised rules on artificial intelligence

URL: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689

Stable ELI reference:

http://data.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj

Relevance:

- binding legal text of the AI Act
- Article 6 defines high-risk classification pathways
- Annex I concerns Union harmonisation legislation for product-related AI systems
- Annex III lists high-risk use-case areas
- Article 14 concerns human oversight
- logging, documentation, risk management and post-market monitoring obligations are relevant to the Refusal Object / Governability Architecture framing

Use for:

- legal basis
- Article 6
- Annex I
- Annex III
- Article 14
- high-risk obligations

## Submission-relevant facts to preserve

The consultation is not a general AI safety consultation.

It is specifically about the draft guidelines for classification of high-risk AI systems.

The key official target is clarity of the guidelines and usefulness of examples.

The VAIG / MECHA / Governability submission should therefore stay focused on:

- Article 6 classification
- execution-path mediation
- refusal and escalation as governance-relevant transition states
- Authority Visibility as a human oversight clarification
- Refusal Object as a transition artifact
- Governability Architecture as transition-legitimacy monitoring

## Working citation notes

Preferred source hierarchy:

1. EU AI Act legal text
2. Commission consultation page
3. Commission draft guidelines page
4. AI Act Service Desk / Single Information Platform
5. Secondary commentary only if needed for context, not authority

Do not cite technology press for core claims when the Commission page already states the deadline, purpose and submission process.
